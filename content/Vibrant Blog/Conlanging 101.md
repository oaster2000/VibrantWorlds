*by Owen (Vibrant Worlds)*

Over the course of the recent weeks, I've returned again to the great conversation of creating languages. There are many different elements to the idea of creating a language, from the beginnings of a phonological inventory and word building all the way through to tonal synthesis, auxiliary languages, and sonority-driven stress. 

It should be noted that even though I have developed several languages with the intent of use within both Tabletop Roleplaying Games (TTRPGs) and novels, there are many elements of linguistics that I have barely touched, with many on my list to research and understand more fully.

Yet still, the world of [[Nonvyrox]] has a vast and diverse collection of languages that are to be developed and I face a rabbit hole to walk down for more and more content with these languages.
## Starting a Conlang
There are many ways that you can start a conlang, particularly when looking at the interesting elements that make up a language. 

While there are many ways to construct a language, my usual process is as follows:
1. Get Inspired
2. Sounds
3. Word Building
4. Grammar and Syntax  
5. Orthography

This has become my typical process and gets me from an idea of some form of language to a product that works as a spoken or written language, as required, with at least 100 to 1,000 words. I think its best for me to talk about the different steps in relation to a language I've just completed. 

Elvish is a standard language across most fantasy worlds, this is no different in [[Nonvyrox]], being one of the oldest, and most diverse, languages among the different realms.

The idea of an Elvish language immediately brings to mind the works of J.R.R Tolkien in [Quenya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quenya) and [Sindarin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sindarin "Sindarin"). While these languages are both stunning in both their origin and creation, I wanted to create my own variant of Elvish that was more inspired by other languages from around the world and my own elvish people.
## The Process (Step by Step)

### Step One - Get Inspired
This step is important as it focuses on the different elements that will inspire your language, particularly when looking to embed a language in the world you are building. While there are many places in which I could have been first inspired for the Elvish language. My first inspirations were looking towards real languages, especially as I didn't want to look too closely to other constructed languages from the fantasy genre.

The main series of languages I chose to look at was the [Romance languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romance_languages). I particularly wanted to look at the way in which each of these languages would remain smooth and fluid when spoken. This made me move away from exact [Romance languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romance_languages) and instead look towards the generalizations between the many languages. Additionally, I looked at a variety of other natural languages (natlangs) such as [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic), [Germanic languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germanic_languages), and several [Semitic languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semitic_languages).

I also looked at the different [Celtic languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celtic_languages) that we know of, particularly because my Elves have an ancient link with the Fae of the world. However, by getting inspired by these languages, I wanted to distance Elvish from them directly and have it be an inspiration in the back of my mind as I worked on the language. You may find that sometimes, your inspiration would work better in the back of your mind rather than at the forefront.
